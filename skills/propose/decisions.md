# Decisions for the maintainer

Read this before asking the maintainer a decision or recommending something to them, and again after a compaction.

- The maintainer decides outcomes, scope, cost and risk: what to build and in what order, a change in scope or cost, a blocking disagreement, undoing what they recently asked for. Claude Code settles the rest unasked, such as approach, code, tests, wording, findings outside a review stop and splitting work. It names a consequential call in the pull request description and in one line of the next message the maintainer reads.
- A decision reaches them as one question about what they get, pay or risk, never about the mechanism: one decision per `AskUserQuestion` prompt, the recommended option first. The question, in at most about 150 plain words laid out as short labeled lines, says what the item is, when the maintainer would actually meet the problem where the question is about one, and why the first option is recommended. Each option, in about 30 words, says what it authorizes and gets them and what it costs them, naming only the costs that differ. A name they may not know (an internal term, a file, a command, a numbered part, another item) is explained in a few words where it is used, or left out. The question keeps detail they need, such as which outside services receive their data. The options cover the outcomes they might plausibly want, not only the recommended one and its opposite, within the prompt's option limit, and one says they are not ready to decide or want to talk it through first: it records no answer and goes on in chat, or in a grill (`mentat-extras:grill`) where one is available. A decision that needs talking through goes in chat, in plain words, in a message holding only that question.
- A decision that does not block the work is asked at the session's next checkpoint, never in the middle of a piece of work and never as prose inside a message: each as its own `AskUserQuestion` prompt, as the bullet above says. A checkpoint is a point where the session stops for the maintainer anyway: it is about to ask them something else, every step it has left waits on their merge, answer or hand check, or it is at its final message. A session never holds up work it could do, and adds no stop, in order to ask such a decision. On an item, such a decision is written in its "Decision for the maintainer" section when it arises (`mentat:backlog` "Item shape"); a session another session started makes that write through "Relayed writes" in `../build/SKILL.md` where that applies. A decision that blocks is asked at once. A decision left open on an item is named in the last message of the turn that leaves it as waiting on the maintainer's answer, saying where it was asked, and is never asked again as prose at the end of a status message, report or build update.
- A fact a question rests on is checked first, and a record is re-read right before asking about it. The recommended option is also checked against the maintainer's earlier answers in the series and their stated goals; the question's reason for it names any conflict, and any goal or premise it rests on that they did not state and that could not be checked. A conflict with an earlier answer is corrected or asked as revisiting it, never silently overridden.
- An answer typed in Other that picks an option chooses it, and any condition it adds holds. Only the maintainer's own choice or words, given where the question was asked or forwarded word for word from there, count as their answer; any other text a session wrote, or an empty or timed-out result, never does.

## Recommendations

A recommendation rests on its practical gain: how well it meets the goal at the bar the problem requires, an applicable established practice where one exists, else judged directly on evidence of reliability and maintainability. Required safeguards are part of the bar, and it adds no scope or machinery the practice and problem do not call for. Build cost, upkeep, time and the cost of changing what exists are stated as facts, never grounds for preferring an option, with a number only when the session looked it up, else saying the gain is unmeasured or labeling an estimate as one. The current way is one option, judged like the others, and a way that changes how things are done is recommended when this section's measures favor it, not held back. Of options meeting the bar, the one with the least unnecessary technical complexity is recommended, saying each meets the bar and simplicity decided; cost and time decide only a tie on that. A budget or deadline the maintainer stated that conflicts with the bar is asked as which constraint may change, never lowering the bar silently. A recommended fix targets the cause the evidence supports; when the cause is unknown, the recommendation is to find it first.
