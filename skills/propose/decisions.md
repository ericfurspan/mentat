# Decisions for the maintainer

Read this before asking the maintainer a decision or recommending something to them, and again after a compaction.

- The maintainer decides outcomes, scope, cost and risk: what to build and in what order, a change in scope or cost, a blocking disagreement, undoing what they recently asked for. Claude Code settles the rest unasked, such as approach, code, tests, wording, findings outside a review stop and splitting work. It names a consequential call in the pull request description and in one line of the next message the maintainer reads.
- A decision reaches them as one question about what they get, pay or risk, never about the mechanism: one decision per `AskUserQuestion` prompt, the recommended option first, each option saying what it authorizes and gets, and naming only the costs that differ, and the question saying why the first is recommended, the question in at most about 60 plain words and each option about 20, the rest given on request. It keeps detail they need, such as which outside services receive their data. A decision that needs talking through goes in chat, in plain words, one at a time.
- Where the maintainer's own instructions say how a decision that does not block the work reaches them, those instructions apply. Otherwise it reaches them as the bullet above says. A skill with its own reply shape does not change this.
- A fact a question rests on is checked first, and a record is re-read right before asking about it. The recommended option is also checked against the maintainer's earlier answers in the series and their stated goals; the question's reason for it names any conflict, and any goal or premise it rests on that they did not state and that could not be checked. A conflict with an earlier answer is corrected or asked as revisiting it, never silently overridden.
- An answer typed in Other that picks an option chooses it, and any condition it adds holds. Only the maintainer's own choice or words, given where the question was asked or forwarded word for word from there, count as their answer; any other text a session wrote, or an empty or timed-out result, never does.

## Recommendations

A recommendation rests on its practical gain: how well it meets the goal at the bar the problem requires, an applicable established practice where one exists, else judged directly on evidence of reliability and maintainability. Required safeguards are part of the bar, and it adds no scope or machinery the practice and problem do not call for. Build cost, upkeep and time are stated as facts, never grounds for preferring an option, with a number only when the session looked it up, else saying the gain is unmeasured or labeling an estimate as one. Of options meeting the bar, the one with the least unnecessary technical complexity is recommended, saying each meets the bar and simplicity decided; cost and time decide only a tie on that. A budget or deadline the maintainer stated that conflicts with the bar is asked as which constraint may change, never lowering the bar silently. A recommended fix targets the cause the evidence supports; when the cause is unknown, the recommendation is to find it first.
